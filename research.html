SpellBattle: UI/UX Competitive Intelligence

10 Jun 2026 · source file

Date: 2026-06-10 Scope: Super Spell Heroes (primary reference), Clash Royale, Hearthstone collection manager, Kingdom Rush Battles, cross-game UX patterns

Takeaway First

Three patterns repeat across every well-regarded game in this space and are strong enough to call industry defaults:

  1. A bottom navigation bar of 3–5 tabs gives players instant one-tap access to every major mode.
  2. The card/spell thumbnail communicates exactly three things at a glance — cost/tier, element/type, and rarity — through color borders and a fixed numeric badge, never prose.
  3. Hero selection lives before deck building in the flow, because the hero defines which cards are legal, and reversing that order forces players to backtrack.

Every implication for SpellBattle follows from how well these three things are executed within its constraints.

1. Super Spell Heroes — Primary Reference

Status and Scale

Built by Sviper GmbH (Hamburg), published by Flaregames, fully released August 2018. Estimated lifetime downloads: 500K–1M. Estimated monthly revenue at late-stage peak: ~$5K/month (Sensor Tower estimate). The game is a niche success, not a breakout — the core design is compelling but it could not escape typical F2P churn pressures.

Core Loop and Match Design

Real-time 1v1 spell-casting duel on a tile board. Each hero is locked to exactly two elements (Fire, Nature, Light, Earth, or Water). You drag from a spell icon to matching element tiles to power it up; longer chains = more powerful spells. Spells have cast times, so a slower spell telegraphs itself and can be blocked.

The opponent's board is visible as a minimap in the top-left corner of your screen — you can see which tiles and spells they are highlighting in real time, enabling reads, anticipation, and bluffs. This mutual visibility is the design's most praised feature.

Deck structure: Four rarity tiers — Basic, Advanced, Elite, Ultimate. Players carry four spells per tier, up to sixteen total. Only one Ultimate spell is allowed per deck. Heroes are fixed to their two-element pairing.

Main Menu Structure

  • Map (zone-based campaign and quest progression)
  • Spell/deck management screen
  • Achievements (trophy icon)
  • Guild functionality
  • Leaderboard
  • Shop (keys, gems, coins)

Navigation uses screen sections/tabs rather than a deep menu tree. Praised for "smooth transitions between screens" and general polish. Critical failure: nine third-party data-sharing dialogs at first launch — a severe onboarding violation.

Deck Builder UI

Accessible from the main menu. Players scroll through collected spells, organized by element and tier, and build loadouts per hero. The visual language is colorful and saturated with heavy element color-coding. Cards use element color heavily — red for Fire, green for Nature, etc.

Known friction: At least three distinct currency types (coins, gems, runes) with no explanation of their distinct uses. A classic F2P complexity trap.

Match UI (reconstructed from reviews and tips)

  • Your full board: lower two-thirds of the portrait screen
  • Opponent minimap: top-left corner, scaled down
  • HP display: drains to zero (confirmed by review text)
  • Hero portrait visible during match
  • Spell drag interface: drag from spell tile to element tiles to chain

Hero System

10 heroes confirmed: Zenron (Sorcerer), Jane (Witch), Thoben (Monk), Tierra (Huntress), Myris (Mage), Asgard (Golem), Van Raven, Lua, Ra'ju, Ray. Each locked to two of five elements. Hero unlock rate was the most consistently criticized aspect — described as "excruciatingly slow."

Player Praise

  • Crisp vibrant graphics, smooth controls, punctual sounds
  • Transition animations between battles and power-ups specifically praised
  • Minimap opponent visibility praised as tactically rich
  • Skill-based: "a canny player can comfortably beat one who has reached a higher level through grinding"

Player Complaints

  • Currency: "confusing, no explanation of how the varying monies work"
  • Hero unlock rate: "excruciatingly slow"
  • Early spell visual variety: "distinct lack of visual variation in early hours"
  • Nine consent dialogs at first launch
  • Upgrade costs increase steeply post-tutorial

2. Clash Royale — Deck Builder Benchmark

Scale

  • 662M+ lifetime downloads; $3.3B+ lifetime revenue
  • 10M monthly active users (average); 2025 revenue: $646.8M (major recovery driven by content updates)
  • Day-1 retention: 44%; Day-30 retention: 15%
  • Average session: 14 minutes, 4 sessions per day

This is the category-defining game. Players entering any PvP mobile card game in 2025–2026 carry its interaction patterns as muscle memory.

Main Menu Structure

Persistent bottom navigation bar as the organizational spine:

  1. Social/Clan (far left)
  2. Cards (collection + deck builder)
  3. Battle (primary CTA — home screen, largest button, center)
  4. Shop
  5. Events/Challenges (far right)

The chest unlock queue sits on the home screen as a persistent row (4 chest slots). Currency and King Level in a top bar. Key principle: every section is within one tap. Non-fullscreen popups let you see the underlying screen, preserving orientation. Never more than one level deep.

Deck Builder UI

  • Your 8 card slots displayed at the top (1–2 rows)
  • Full card collection as a scrollable grid below
  • Tap a card to slot it; tap a slotted card to swap
  • Auto-advances the cursor through empty slots in a single continuous flow
  • Filters: All / Troops / Spells / Buildings (type); sort by Rarity or Elixir Cost
  • Cards already in the deck are grayed out in the collection — cannot accidentally double-add
  • Average Elixir Cost displayed as a summary stat for the current deck
  • Multiple saved deck slots, switchable via numbered tabs

Card Thumbnail Anatomy

  • Character/spell art fills the card face
  • Elixir cost: colored circle/badge, bottom-center of card face
  • Card name: below or within the art
  • Rarity: communicated via border/background color — Common (gray), Rare (orange), Epic (purple), Legendary (gold/rainbow), Champion (animated red/gold)
  • Level: small number on the card
  • Upgrade progress: duplicates needed shown as a progress element

The rarity color system is consistent enough that players sort their collection by color without reading text.

Battle UI

  • Opponent's arena at the top half; player's arena at the bottom half
  • Player's card hand: 4 cards at the very bottom, always visible
  • Elixir bar: purple progress bar (0–10), directly above the card hand
  • Timer: center-top, counting down from 3:00
  • Tower HP: shown on each structure in the arena
  • Cards deployed by drag from hand to arena position
  • Interaction point lives in the bottom ~20% of screen — reachable with one thumb

Key UX Principles

  • Yellow = primary action; Green = secondary positive; Red = notifications
  • Shop: never more than 6 items at once (avoids paradox of choice)
  • Max 1 notification popup at startup
  • All key interface elements within the lower 50% of screen

3. Hearthstone Collection Manager — Card Anatomy Reference

Not a portrait-mode brawler, but the card anatomy is the reference point for the entire category.

Card Thumbnail Anatomy (confirmed)

  • Mana cost: top-left, inside a blue crystal badge — the largest number on the card face
  • Attack (minions): bottom-left, inside a yellow sword badge
  • Health (minions): bottom-right, inside a red drop badge
  • Rarity gem: bottom-center of portrait, colored by rarity — Common (white/gray), Rare (blue), Epic (purple), Legendary (orange)
  • Card name: centered band across the middle
  • Card text: lower third

Key principle: stats "pop outside the frame" — the mana gem and stat badges break out of the card border so they remain legible even when cards overlap or are partially obscured.

Collection Manager

  • Class tabs at the top (icon-based, not text labels)
  • Mana cost filter along the bottom (numbered icons 0–10+)
  • Cards in deck: outlined in blue; cards not owned: near-invisible gray
  • Deck sidebar: card name + mana cost per card, with a mana curve histogram

Deck Validity Feedback

Clear "deck is ready" state (name field editable, "Done" button activates) vs. incomplete state (slot count shown, cards still needed). No ambiguity. The gold standard for deck completion feedback.

4. Kingdom Rush Battles — Typed Slots Reference

Useful secondary comparison: portrait-mode PvP card battler in the casual strategy space.

Deck Builder Structure

  • Navigate to Collection tab (three-card icon, left side of screen)
  • Deck has fixed typed slots: 1 Hero + 3 Spells + 4 Towers
  • Tap a card → select "Use" to add it to the current deck
  • "Info" option shows full card description, stats, rank, type, and skills
  • Up to 3 saved decks simultaneously, switchable via numbered tabs (1, 2, 3)
  • Sort by rank or rarity

The typed-slot model (one hero slot, three spell slots, four tower slots) makes deck composition immediately legible: you always know what kind of slot you're filling. This is a direct structural parallel to SpellBattle's hero + tiered spell deck concept.

Card Thumbnail — Minimal Legible Design

A card thumbnail must answer three questions without the player reading text:

  1. What tier/cost is this? — A single prominent badge or number (mana gem, elixir circle, tier label)
  2. What element/type is this? — Color of the card background or border
  3. How rare is this? — A distinct rarity color or gem distinct from the element color

Name and art fill the remaining space. Everything else (description, stats, level) lives in a detail panel, not the thumbnail.

Hero Selection

In every multi-hero game surveyed:

  • Hero selection happens before deck building — hero determines which cards are available
  • Heroes shown as large portrait-style cards in a horizontal scroll or grid of 2–4 visible at once
  • Hero portraits communicate identity immediately through art and color palette
  • Locked heroes are visible but grayed out, creating aspiration

Battle UI (Portrait 1v1)

  • Opponent at top, player at bottom — inverting this would confuse players, it is that universal
  • Player action area at the bottom — reachable with one thumb
  • Resource bar (elixir, mana, cast progress) just above the action area
  • HP/health flanking the top (or on structures in the arena)
  • Timer in the center-top, visible but not dominant
  • The battle screen is the one screen where UI elements spread across the full portrait height

6. Implications for SpellBattle

Hero selection is the first navigation choice, not an afterthought

The universal pattern: hero before deck. Tapping "Collection" from the main menu should first show the hero roster, then drop into that hero's spell loadout. This also makes it natural to filter the spell pool to that hero's two elements. Players coming from Clash Royale or Super Spell Heroes will not be confused — it matches expectation.

Spell cards need two distinct badge types: tier/level badge + element background; rarity is the border

SpellBattle's four rarity tiers (Basic / Rare / Epic / Legendary, unlocking at match levels 1–4) map directly onto the industry's four-tier rarity system. Use the border or background color for rarity, a separate element color tint for the card face, and a small tier label ("L1" through "L4" or the rarity name) in one corner. Spell name and art fill the center. Description goes in a tap-to-expand detail panel only.

Deck builder: typed slot rows per tier, one row per rarity

Show four rows at the top — one per tier (Basic, Rare, Epic, Legendary) — each holding the max number of that rarity's spells. Filled slots show the card thumbnail; empty slots show a placeholder with the tier label. When a row is full, cards of that tier are dimmed in the collection below. This makes the "only one Legendary" constraint visually self-evident.

Main menu: 3 tabs only, battle button dominant

Right now SpellBattle needs: Play (queue match), Collection (hero roster → per-hero deck), Shop (deferred). The Play button should be the largest centered element. Design the Collection tab now to support the hero-first flow rather than retrofitting it later when hero selection comes.

Match screen: your board fills the bottom 60%, opponent minimap top-left, HP flanking the top

Super Spell Heroes validated this layout and players praised it. SpellBattle already has independent boards as a design constraint. The binary shield state (full block vs. broken with overflow) needs a distinct visual state — a shield icon fully opaque (active) or shattered/absent (broken). Do not show partial shield progress; the binary nature is load-bearing and should be reinforced visually.

Core loop

Players draw paths across an elemental board to chain spells. Sessions are short (2–4 min) and built around one more match tension. Key hooks:

  • Daily chest timers that reward returning
  • Hero mastery progression per element
  • A ladder with weekly reset

Monetization snapshot

HeroesSoft + hard currencyGated behind ladder tiers
ChestsTimed / paid skipClassic CR-style funnel
Battle passSeasonalCosmetic + currency track

Implications for SpellBattle

  1. Your level cap of 4 keeps matches tight — lean into that as a differentiator.
  2. A spell-as-fuel system rewards deck cohesion; surface that in onboarding.
  3. Avoid pay-to-win on heroes — sell identity, not power, to fit "heroes define identity".
